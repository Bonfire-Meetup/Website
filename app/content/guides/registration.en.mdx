<Sections>

<Section title="Email + code flow" icon="mail">

1. Open login and enter your email.
2. Complete the human check and request a one-time code.
3. Check your inbox and enter the code on the same login screen.
4. Codes are short-lived and one-time; if it expires, request a new one.

</Section>

<Section title="When registration happens" icon="user">

1. There is no separate registration page.
2. After a valid email code is confirmed, your account is created (or reused if it already exists).
3. Then you are signed in and redirected to your account page.

</Section>

<Section title="We value your privacy" icon="lock">

Out of the gate, you're opted out of comms and your profile stays private. No rush, you can change both whenever you're ready.

</Section>

<Section title="How passkeys fit in" icon="fingerprint">

1. First, sign in with email code at least once.
2. Open Account and add a passkey in Security.
3. Next time, use the passkey button on login for faster sign-in on supported devices.

<InfoPanel icon="shield" title="Why passkeys are safer">

Passkeys reduce common account-takeover risks compared to one-time codes and passwords.

<Steps variant="emerald-dot" size="sm">

- No shared secret to type, so phishing pages cannot steal your password.
- Cryptographic login is bound to your device and verified against the real domain.
- Biometrics or device PIN add local user verification before sign-in.

</Steps>

</InfoPanel>

</Section>

</Sections>

<Cta title="Good to know" primaryHref="/login" primaryLabel="Open login" secondaryHref="/me" secondaryLabel="Open account">

If login fails, request a fresh code. You can always continue with email code even if passkey is unavailable on a device.

</Cta>
